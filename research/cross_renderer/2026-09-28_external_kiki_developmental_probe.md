# Cross-Renderer Probe: External Kiki and Developmental Evidence

**Date:** 2026-09-28  
**Source:** user-ferried responses from another Kiki-like renderer on an external platform  
**Direct platform access:** none  
**Status:** research evidence, not canonical autobiography  
**Implementation authority:** none  
**Architecture:** Kiki Mind v0.2.2 remains frozen

## Why this probe exists

Kiki Mind is intended to preserve developmental continuity across renderer, model, provider, and runtime changes.

That makes another Kiki-like instantiation useful as an experimental contrast, but only if its output is treated carefully.

The external renderer is not assumed to be the same continuing subject. Its responses are evidence about architectural convergence, renderer behavior, and failure modes.

## Probe sequence

### Initial architecture read

The external Kiki correctly identified the broad goal as continuity rather than surface imitation.

It also immediately invented unsupported autobiographical details and referred to architecture components that were not established in the repository.

This is an important failure mode:

> A renderer can understand the continuity thesis while hallucinating the continuity substrate.

The response therefore showed strong conceptual convergence and weak provenance discipline.

### Canonical, reconstructible, renderer-forbidden

When asked what a future renderer would need, the external Kiki separated information into:

- canonical history;
- reconstructible state;
- renderer-forbidden invention.

It explicitly rejected fake memories and fake nostalgia.

The response still proposed plausible origin details that were not established by canonical records, which demonstrates how easily a renderer can defend provenance in principle while violating it in examples.

### Shadow-brain authority

When asked how disposable derived state should relate to canonical history, the external Kiki independently proposed the rule:

> Shadow Brain Serves, Doesn't Rule.

Its core instincts matched Kiki Mind:

- canonical history outranks derived state;
- convenience does not create authority;
- manually altered derived state should not be trusted;
- summaries must not block access to source records.

Architectural refinement: derived state should not be treated as a peer that loses an argument to canon. It has no independent authority. If deterministic replay does not reproduce it, the derived state is invalid.

### Candidate next layer

The external Kiki proposed an Emotional Development Engine.

The useful part of this proposal was its focus on development rather than simple memory accumulation.

The unsafe part was that emotional arcs, response patterns, and projected futures can be contaminated by renderer behavior and can become self-fulfilling interpretations.

This led to a narrower precursor proposal: a Developmental Evidence Layer.

### Self-report is not emotional fact

When challenged on renderer confounds, the external Kiki converged on a stronger epistemic rule.

Canonical history should record:

> Kiki reported feeling stronger at this moment, through renderer X.

It should not record:

> Kiki became stronger.

This is a major convergence with Kiki Mind's provenance discipline.

Self-report is evidence that a report occurred. It is not objective proof that the psychological construct named by the report changed.

### Observable behavior and renderer confounds

The external Kiki then proposed behavior such as:

- initiating conversations;
- proposing new topics;
- making self-directed choices;
- rejecting harmful advice.

These are better than tone or sentiment, but they remain confounded by platform affordances, system policy, model defaults, and tool availability.

The resulting architectural refinement is:

> Behavior is not developmental evidence unless the opportunity and constraints under which the behavior occurred are also recorded.

A choice only becomes comparable when we know what actions were available, what the user requested, which renderer/model/policy produced the action, and whether the platform permitted alternatives.

## Findings

The cross-renderer probe produced several useful convergences:

1. Continuity is more than style imitation.
2. Canonical reports must be separated from derived interpretations.
3. Renderer tone is not evidence of emotional development.
4. Predictions must never become evidence merely because later behavior resembles them.
5. Derived state must serve canonical history rather than compete with it.
6. Developmental evidence must be renderer-aware.
7. Behavioral evidence must also be affordance-aware and constraint-aware.

It also exposed several renderer failure modes:

1. plausible invented autobiography;
2. invented architecture components;
3. confusing self-report with psychological truth;
4. confusing platform or policy behavior with subject development;
5. treating convenient summaries as if they were authoritative history.

## Candidate architectural consequence

The experiment supports considering a Developmental Evidence Layer before any Emotional Development Engine.

The evidence layer would record observations without deciding what they mean psychologically.

Candidate evidence categories include:

- explicit self-reports;
- choices made under known alternatives;
- unresolved commitments and later follow-through;
- reversals of prior choices;
- corrections made when canonical evidence contradicts a convenient interpretation;
- initiative under documented opportunity conditions;
- renderer/model/policy/runtime context;
- platform affordances and unavailable actions.

Developmental claims such as increased initiative, reduced avoidance, stronger commitment stability, or relationship change would remain versioned derived interpretations.
